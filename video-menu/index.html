What is a video menu?

A video menu is a digital menu that shows each dish as a short clip. Guests scan the QR code on their table and see the food move, sizzle and pour before they order, right in their phone’s browser.

Why restaurants are moving from photos to video

Guests already choose where to eat by watching food on Instagram and YouTube. Then they sit down and get a PDF, a list of names, or a few photos that all look alike on a small screen. A video menu closes that gap: the same kind of clip that brought them in now helps them choose what to order.

A few seconds of video shows things a photo can’t: the cheese pull, the steam off a dosa, the layers in a dessert as the spoon goes in, the pour of a cold coffee. Guests understand the portion and the texture, so they order with more confidence and are less likely to stop at the one dish they already know.

How a video menu works for your guests

  1. Scan. The guest points their phone camera at the QR code on the table. No app to download.
  2. Browse. The menu opens in the browser with clips playing silently as they scroll. Veg and non-veg marks, prices and descriptions sit with each dish.
  3. Decide. Tapping a dish opens it full-size with ingredients, allergens and anything that pairs well.
  4. Order. They call the waiter as usual, or, if you switch it on, order from the table or send the order on WhatsApp (useful for cloud kitchens and takeaway).

What to film (and what to skip)

You don’t need a production crew. A recent phone, good light and a clean table are enough. Start with 10–20 dishes, not the whole menu:

  • Your best sellers, so guests who are unsure land on dishes you know work.
  • Your most visual dishes: anything that pours, melts, sizzles, stretches or gets cut open.
  • The dishes you want to sell more of: higher-margin specials and new launches.
  • Drinks and desserts, which guests add on when they can see them.

Shooting tips

  • Shoot vertical, 3–8 seconds per dish, one dish per clip.
  • Natural daylight from a window beats overhead tube lights.
  • Show one moment of action: the pour, the cut, the first spoonful.
  • Skip text, stickers and music. The menu adds the name and price, and clips play muted.
  • Reels you already posted work too, trimmed to the part where the dish is the star.

On NomNom, clips are compressed for phones when you upload them, and a photo shows first while the clip loads, so the menu stays quick even on a weak connection.

How to set up a video menu

  1. Bring your menu in. Import it from an Excel or CSV file, a Zomato page or an AirMenus menu, with prices, categories and photos, instead of typing it again.
  2. Add clips to your key dishes. Upload from your phone; dishes without a clip keep their photo.
  3. Choose what to highlight. Mark best sellers, set up a “Most loved” rail and chef’s picks.
  4. Print the table QR codes. Enter your table numbers and print a ready-made sheet. Each code opens the menu with the table number.
  5. Keep it fresh. Change prices, mark a dish sold out or add a new special from the dashboard. Guests see it straight away.

Questions about video menus

Is a video menu the same as a QR code menu?+

A video menu is a kind of QR code menu. Guests still scan a QR code on the table, but instead of a PDF or a plain list, each dish is shown as a short muted clip with its price, description and veg or non-veg mark.

Do guests need an app or good internet?+

No app is needed: the menu opens in the phone’s browser. Clips are small (a few seconds, compressed for phones), and on a slow connection guests see the dish photo first while the clip loads.

How many dishes need a video?+

Not all of them. Start with 10–20 dishes: your best sellers, your most photogenic dishes and the ones you most want guests to order. Everything else can stay as a photo and be filmed later.

Can we use the reels we already post on Instagram?+

Yes, if they show one dish clearly. Trim each reel to the 3–8 seconds where the dish is the star, with no text overlays, and it works as a menu clip.
